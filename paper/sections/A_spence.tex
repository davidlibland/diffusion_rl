\section{The Spence loss: derivation and implementation}
\label{sec:A_spence}

\subsection{From the gradient weight to the potential}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:minimizer}, a Bregman loss in log space reaches the
optimizer only through the weight $w(u)=u\varphi''(u)$ in
\eqref{eq:weight-grad}. We therefore specify $w$ and recover $\varphi$, rather
than the other way round.

Requirements \eqref{eq:req-over}--\eqref{eq:req-under} ask that the gradient
grow linearly in $p=\ln u$ in both directions, giving $w(u)\sim \ln u/u$ as
$u\to\infty$ and $w(u)\sim-\ln u$ as $u\to0$. The choice
\begin{equation}
  w(u)=\frac{\ln u}{u-1},
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \varphi''(u)=\frac{w(u)}{u}=\frac{\ln u}{u\,(u-1)},
  \label{eq:a-phi2}
\end{equation}
meets both. It is positive on all of $(0,\infty)$, since $\ln u$ and $u-1$
change sign together at $u=1$, and the singularity there is removable with
$w(1)=1$. Equivalently $w(u)=1/L(1,u)$, the reciprocal of the logarithmic mean
$L(a,b)=(b-a)/(\ln b-\ln a)$.

Integrating \eqref{eq:a-phi2} twice gives the potential
\begin{equation}
  \varphi(u) \;=\; (1-u)\,\Spence(1-u) \;-\; \tfrac12\,u\ln^{2}u .
  \label{eq:a-phi}
\end{equation}
The two constants of integration are immaterial: a Bregman divergence is
unchanged by adding an affine function of $u$ to its potential, since the
added terms cancel between $\varphi(T)-\varphi(U)$ and $\varphi'(U)(T-U)$.

\subsection{Closed forms}

Write $S(x)=\Spence(1-e^{x})$. Substituting $u=e^{p}$ for the prediction and
$T=e^{q}$ for the target into $\Breg(T,u)$ with \eqref{eq:a-phi} gives the
divergence in closed form,
\begin{equation}
  L(p,q) \;=\; \Breg\big(e^{q},e^{p}\big)
  \;=\; \tfrac12 e^{q}\big(p^{2}-q^{2}\big)
        \;+\;\big(e^{q}-1\big)\big(S(p)-S(q)\big),
  \label{eq:a-value}
\end{equation}
which is the expression the implementation reports as the monitored loss
value. Differentiating in $p$ and using the dilogarithm identity
\begin{equation}
  S'(x) \;=\; \Spence'(1-e^{x})\cdot(-e^{x})
        \;=\; -\,\frac{x\,e^{x}}{e^{x}-1}
        \;=\; \frac{x}{\mathrm{expm1}(-x)},
  \label{eq:a-Sprime}
\end{equation}
which follows from $\Spence'(z)=-\ln(1-z)/z$, the cross terms cancel and leave
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial p}
  \;=\; e^{q}p + \big(e^{q}-1\big)S'(p)
  \;=\; p\,\frac{e^{p}-e^{q}}{e^{p}-1},
  \label{eq:a-grad}
\end{equation}
recovering \eqref{eq:spence-grad}. It is this appearance of the Spence
function in \eqref{eq:a-value} that names the loss.

All four identities of this subsection --- \eqref{eq:a-phi2} as the second
derivative of \eqref{eq:a-phi}, \eqref{eq:a-value} as $\Breg(e^{q},e^{p})$,
\eqref{eq:a-grad} as its $p$-derivative, and \eqref{eq:a-Sprime} --- are
verified symbolically in the accompanying code.

\subsection{Numerical implementation}
\label{subsec:a-numerics}

Training never evaluates \eqref{eq:a-value}. The backward pass returns
\eqref{eq:a-grad} directly, which matters: differentiating \eqref{eq:a-value}
by automatic differentiation returns $\mathrm{NaN}$ in single precision once
the \emph{target} reaches $q\gtrsim85$ nats, and again when prediction and
target are both large (e.g.\ $p=q=100$), because the $e^{q}$ prefactor of
\eqref{eq:a-value} overflows and multiplies an intermediate that has itself
lost precision. Large predictions alone are harmless on this path --- $p=300$
with $q=0$ differentiates correctly --- so the failure is driven by the
target, which is exactly the quantity the learner does not control. A single
such batch propagates $\mathrm{NaN}$ through the shared value network and
poisons every subsequent update.

Even in closed form, \eqref{eq:a-grad} must not be evaluated literally: both
$e^{p}-e^{q}$ and $e^{p}-1$ overflow. We compute it branch-wise,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial p} =
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle p\,\frac{\mathrm{expm1}(p)-\mathrm{expm1}(q)}{\mathrm{expm1}(p)},
      & p<0,\\[2.2ex]
    \displaystyle p\,\frac{\mathrm{expm1}(q-p)}{\mathrm{expm1}(-p)}, & p>0,\\[2.2ex]
    -\,\mathrm{expm1}(q), & p=0,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:a-branches}
\end{equation}
which is finite for every finite float input. For $p<0$, $\mathrm{expm1}(p)$
lies in $[-1,0)$ and the difference of \texttt{expm1} values is free of
cancellation, while an underflowing $e^{q}$ degrades gracefully to $0$. For
$p>0$, factoring $e^{p}$ out of numerator and denominator leaves both
\texttt{expm1} arguments bounded above, so nothing overflows, and the
expression tends to the true asymptote $p$. The $p=0$ case is the removable
singularity, $\lim_{p\to0}p/\mathrm{expm1}(p)=1$. A final guard maps any
residual non-finite value to $\pm10^{30}$; it is reachable only when the true
gradient itself exceeds the float range, e.g.\ $e^{q}=\infty$, where training
is already pathological and a large finite gradient at least gives Adam a
correctly-signed step.

\subsection{Where the Spence weight sits}

The three weights of Table~\ref{tab:loss-family} are ordered at both extremes:
as $u\to\infty$, $1/u < \ln u/(u-1) < 2u$, and as $u\to0^{+}$,
$2u < \ln u/(u-1) < 1/u$. In this asymptotic sense the Spence loss
interpolates squared error and Itakura--Saito rather than introducing an
unrelated third behaviour. We state this only asymptotically on purpose: the
inequality is not global, failing in a narrow band around $u\approx0.6$, where
$w$ dips slightly below $1/u$.
